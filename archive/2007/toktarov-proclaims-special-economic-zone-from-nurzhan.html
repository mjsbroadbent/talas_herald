Toktarov Proclaims Special Economic Zone from Nurzhan

The former Senate majority leader releases what he says are secret drafts to divide Irad among its neighbors, declares an emergency administration and annuls the Supreme Court's independence ruling; the cabinet in Talas calls the proclamation unlawful.

By Olga Fedorenko · Monday 26 February 2007

NURZHAN — Erlan Toktarov, the former Majority Leader of the Territorial Senate, yesterday released documents that he said were drafts of treaties under which the government of Prime Minister Bakyt Orozbekov would dissolve the Democratic Republic and divide Irad among Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan. Reading passages to a crowd of several thousand on the central square in Nurzhan, he called the documents "a bill of sale for our country", and his aides handed out photocopies.

Mr. Toktarov then proclaimed an "Irad Special Economic Zone" and said it would govern as an emergency administration. Reading a decree at the city administration building, he declared the Supreme Court's independence ruling of December annulled and said that Irad had returned to "suspended sovereignty" pending a referendum on unification. "A country with no sovereign cannot be sold," he told the crowd.

Mr. Toktarov said he had taken control of the Democratic Party of Irad. He had warned in December against the immediate proclamation of the Democratic Republic, and he accused the cabinet of selling the country's assets "by auction to its friends" and of preparing to sell the country itself. Municipal police lent by the Nurzhan city council, where the Labor Party holds the majority, and gendarmerie units from the Chu lowlands took up positions last night around the city administration, the rail yards and the NurGaz tank farm. Labor councillors said they backed the move to restore order.

In Talas, a spokesman for Mr. Orozbekov said the cabinet did not recognize the proclamation, which he called "a seizure of power with no basis in law", and that the government of the Democratic Republic remained the lawful authority. He said the documents were contingency papers prepared by officials during consultations on borders and water, that no minister had approved them, and that their authenticity had not been established. The proclamation follows weeks of protest over the cabinet's sale of oil concessions, grain elevators and mobile frequencies, which documents published by the Irad Times link to companies close to ministers and other party members. NurGaz, whose president Kamran Bekzatov has refused to recognize the oil auctions, made no comment last night.

The Herald has obtained a set of the documents and reports on their contents tomorrow.
