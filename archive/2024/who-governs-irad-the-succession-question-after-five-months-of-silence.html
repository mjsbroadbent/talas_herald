Who Governs Irad? The Succession Question After Five Months of Silence

Five months after the Governor's last confirmed public appearance, no formal mechanism has been invoked, and lawyers disagree on whether one is needed.

By Nadia Osmonalieva · Saturday 7 December 2024

TALAS — Five months have passed since Governor Erlan Toktarov's last confirmed public appearance in early July. The DPI says he is on an extended health retreat and continues to govern, and decrees and recorded addresses are still issued in his name. Lieutenant Governor Aidar Maksat chairs the cabinet and receives foreign delegations, and official statements describe him as acting on the Governor's instructions. No journalist is known to have met the Governor since July.

The government's position is that no transfer of authority has taken place. The 1992 Elective Governor Acts provide that the Lieutenant Governor acts for an absent or incapacitated Governor and succeeds if the office falls vacant, but they do not say who decides that a Governor is unable to act, or whether a formal declaration is needed first. Legal scholars are divided, and the provision has never been tested. The legislature, where the DPI holds 51 of 60 seats, has not debated the matter, and Russo-aligned deputies from Nurzhan have called without success for a hearing on the Governor's health.

Analysts note that the only formal routes to a transfer of power are the Acts' succession provisions and the election due in November 2027, and that the DPI has named no successor. The Governor's eldest sons, Ruslan and Alikhan Toktarov, have appeared in public far more often since July, and commentators in Talas openly discuss friction between them. Opposition outlets and technology monitors allege signs of digital synthesis in the Governor's broadcasts; officials reject this as foreign-sponsored disinformation without naming a country, and no evidence of a Russian role has been made public.
