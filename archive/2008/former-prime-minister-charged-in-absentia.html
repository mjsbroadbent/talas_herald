Courts

Former prime minister charged in absentia

Prosecutors accuse Bakyt Orozbekov, who has lived in Istanbul since last year, of high treason over the partition drafts made public in February 2007 and of abuse of office over the privatization auctions.

By Elmira Beishenova · Wednesday 19 March 2008

TALAS — ISEZ prosecutors yesterday charged Bakyt Orozbekov, the only Prime Minister of the Democratic Republic of Irad, in absentia with high treason and with abuse of office. The treason charge concerns the draft treaties to divide Irad among Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan that Erlan Toktarov made public in Nurzhan on 25 February last year, known in Iradi as the Satuu Kagazy. The second charge concerns the sale of state assets by Mr. Orozbekov's cabinet in January and February 2007.

The charge sheet, filed with the Talas District Court, alleges that Mr. Orozbekov directed negotiations on the cession of Iradi territory to foreign states without the knowledge of the national assembly, and that companies tied to him and to several ministers were to receive concession shares in the oil fields, the antimony deposits and the Toktogul dam. It also cites the auctions of oil and antimony exploration concessions, state grain elevators and blocks of mobile-telephone spectrum, and the cabinet's decree privatizing communal pastures in the Chatkal valley. A spokesman for the Attorney General said the office would ask the Turkish authorities for Mr. Orozbekov's return. He declined to say whether former ministers would also be charged. The Iradi Freedom Society, whose forerunner, the Popular Front, demanded Mr. Orozbekov's trial last March, said the charges were "a year late".

Mr. Orozbekov, 45, was arrested at his official residence when the Popular Front stormed Talas on 4 June last year, signed an instrument of resignation and was handed over to a United Nations compound outside the city. He left Irad later in the year under arrangements made by UN officials and now lives in Istanbul. In a statement issued there through his office, he called the charges political. He said the drafts were "contingency papers" that had never been approved by the cabinet, and repeated that the auctions had been open. He has previously denied any personal involvement in the transactions that were later criticized.

The court has not set a date for a hearing. Mr. Orozbekov's lawyers in Talas said they had not yet received the charge sheet.
